Dipak Munshi

I pull faint signals out of large, noisy, instrument-limited data — and build the code that does it at scale.

Imperial College London · London, UK

Profile

Research scientist with nearly 30 years extracting faint signals from large, noisy, instrument-limited datasets, 25 of them in the UK. Core team member on two ESA flagship satellite missions — Planck and Euclid — where I built, tested and integrated analysis pipelines, including the end-to-end validation required before release to real survey data.

I work across the full stack: deriving the method, implementing it in performant code, and running it at scale on HPC and GPU. Around 200 peer-reviewed papers.

Current work

A complete Bayesian treatment of satellite radio-frequency interference in radio interferometry: instrument and beam modelling, orbit determination from interferometric visibilities, detection of uncatalogued emitters, optimal scheduling, and quantified science impact — delivered as self-contained technical reports with reproducible code.

Satellite constellations are now the limiting systematic for low-frequency radio astronomy, and unintended broadband emission from spacecraft electronics falls inside the SKA-Low band while being covered by no spectrum allocation at all. Flagging throws the data away; the work here is on modelling the interference and subtracting it instead.
